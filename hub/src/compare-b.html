Look B against B2

B2 is the same Ink & Wash look, drawn at the screen's full size, with clean pen lines and more drawn detail inside the washes. It costs about twice as much to draw ({{ratio}} times B).

Waiting on Luc

Compare B with B2, then pick one and tell Claude in chat: “B” or “B2”. Menus, lobby and HUD are the same in both.

Play B livePlay B2 liveB2 with its frame-rate readout

  • What changed, in one line: B2 no longer resamples the picture or caps it below the screen's size, its pen lines are anti-aliased, and every wash has pen work fixed to the object it sits on.
  • Sharp: B drew 76% of a 2560×1440 screen and 34% of a 3840×2160 one. B2 draws 100% of both, so nothing is stretched.
  • Detail: wood grain, bark, needles, shingles, rock beds and cracks, pebbles, tide lines, reeds, stitched patches, nails, rope lay, stencils, a far shore.
  • Steady: every stroke rides on its object. Line boil stays off. On land with a still camera, 0 pixels change.
  • Cost: B2 is heavier. If frames run long it sheds reflections first, then fine strokes, then pen anti-aliasing, and resolution last. The &fps=1 readout names whatever it shed.

How to read this page

  • 100% crops: never scaled by this page. On a dense screen (a laptop at 2×) the browser doubles every pixel, for B and B2 alike.
  • Pictures: JPEG, quality 90 for the full shots and 95 for the crops. The original PNG screenshots are on the Mac in scratch/portage/b2/.
  • Frame cost: the frame is drawn 6 times back to back and timed. Other 3D pages were running on the same graphics chip, so both figures read high. Trust the ratio, not the milliseconds.
  • Edge sharpness: at each strong edge, the share of the change that happens in one pixel step. 1.0 is a one-pixel edge. Anti-aliased lines sit near 0.75.
  • Paper grain: the one thing still fixed to the screen. It is the sheet. B2 turns it down from 60 to 36 on the settings slider.
